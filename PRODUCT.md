# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Fresno and Central Valley residents, visitors, families, students, nightlife planners, and culture seekers who want one trustworthy place to find what is happening nearby. The owner/operator also needs a practical curation workflow for adding, reviewing, editing, and publishing events while automated ingestion matures.

## Product Purpose

Fresno Events consolidates scattered local event listings into one common database and turns them into a simple public discovery experience. The product should make it easy to answer: what is worth doing today, what is happening this week, and where can I get the full details or tickets? Success means people can confidently plan around Fresno events without checking five separate calendars or ticketing sites.

## Brand Personality

Playful, community-first, warm. The interface should feel alive and local without becoming loud, gimmicky, or hard to scan.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI/SaaS aesthetics: purple gradients, glassy card piles, bland icon-card grids, dark glows, and visual choices that could fit any startup. Avoid ticketing-site clutter: ads, cramped filters, too many competing calls to action, and confusing pricing or availability treatments. Avoid a nightlife-only mood: the product needs to work for families, daytime events, civic events, arts, food, sports, and community gatherings.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the next useful decision. Home, list, and detail pages should help people decide what to do next without forcing them to decode the system.
2. Keep the Valley in the product. Use local context, neighborhoods, venues, time, and event texture to make the interface feel grounded in Fresno rather than generic.
3. Calm structure, warm personality. The information architecture should be predictable and fast, while copy, imagery, and small moments can carry community warmth.
4. Make trust visible. Show source links, ticket links, venue details, event times, and update/review signals clearly so users know what is real and current.
5. Design for incomplete data. Scraped and manually entered events will vary in quality, so the UI must gracefully handle missing images, partial descriptions, unknown prices, and external-only details.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for public surfaces. Preserve keyboard navigation, visible focus states, reduced-motion behavior, readable mobile text, 44px touch targets, strong contrast over images, and color-blind-safe status cues. The product should work equally well for quick mobile browsing, family planning, and longer desktop exploration.
